## What is the EES and does it apply to Albanian citizens?

The EES (Entry/Exit System) is the European Union’s electronic system that records the entries and exits of non-EU nationals at the external borders of the Schengen area. It started gradually on 12 October 2025 and, according to the European Commission, has been fully operational since 10 April 2026, replacing the stamping of passports.

At the border the system records travel document data (name, date of birth, nationality), a facial image and fingerprints, together with the date and place of every entry and exit, including refusals of entry.

The EES applies to non-EU nationals on short stays, up to 90 days in any 180-day period, in the participating countries. It covers both travellers who need a visa and those who do not. Albania is not among the exempt categories, such as nationals of the participating countries and holders of residence permits or long-stay visas. Albanian citizens travelling to the Schengen area for a holiday, business trip or short visit are therefore within scope. This conclusion comes from the official list of exemptions; we did not find an official page that names Albania explicitly.

## Does the EES change the 90/180-day rule?

No. The rule is the same: you may stay up to 90 days in any 180-day period, counted together across all Schengen countries. What changes is how days are counted. Instead of reading stamps in a passport, the system calculates entries and exits itself and can flag an overstay automatically.

In practice every entry and exit is stored and visible to border authorities. If you plan several trips in one year, keep a simple note of your dates.

## What is ETIAS and will Albanian citizens need it?

ETIAS (European Travel Information and Authorisation System) is a travel authorisation, requested online before travel, for nationals of countries that do not need a visa. It is not a visa and does not collect biometrics. According to the EU Delegation to Albania, all Albanian citizens are eligible for ETIAS because Albania has visa-free travel. Once the system starts, Albanian travellers will need it for short stays in the Schengen area.

## When will ETIAS start?

As of 24 November 2026 no start date has been announced by the EU. The official EU site says the system is “currently not in operation”, that no applications are being collected, and that the EU will announce the start date several months before launch. The Commission’s earlier target was the end of 2026. A Polish government notice dated 18 September 2026 says the launch is planned for 2027, with the date to be communicated later. That is a national source, not a start date announced by the EU, so we do not treat it as a confirmed date.

Practical advice: do not plan anything on the assumption that ETIAS starts on a particular date. We will update this article when a date is announced.

## How much does ETIAS cost and how long is it valid?

In July 2025 the European Commission announced that the fee will be 20 euros, instead of the 7 euros planned earlier, with applicants under 18 and over 70 exempt. The EU Delegation to Albania page, dated 2021, still shows 7 euros; it is out of date.

According to the same page the authorisation is valid for 3 years and allows multiple entries. The Commission says applications are mostly processed automatically, with a decision within minutes; some cases go to manual review.

## The difference at a glance

| Aspect | EES | ETIAS |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Photo and fingerprints recorded at the border | Travel authorisation requested online before travel |
| Status on 24 November 2026 | Fully in force since 10 April 2026 | Not in operation; start date not announced |
| When it happens | At the border, during the check | Before departure, on the official website |
| Who it applies to | Non-EU nationals on short stays | Nationals of visa-free countries, including Albania |

## Beware of fake websites

According to ABTA, quoted by Travelweek in October 2026, more than 150 look-alike websites present themselves as ETIAS. Because the system is not yet open, any site that accepts an ETIAS application or payment today is not official. The real application will only be made on the official EU portal, travel-europe.europa.eu. If you are unsure about a website, do not pay and do not enter your passport details.

## Can a travel agency make the application for you?

The official ETIAS site provides for a traveller to nominate a third party or a commercial intermediary, such as a travel agency, to apply on their behalf. The intermediary must use the official website, and the official fee is separate from any service fee. This option only applies once the system has launched; before that date nobody can apply for ETIAS.

## What to do now

1. Check that your passport is biometric and valid for long enough to cover your whole trip.
2. Do not pay anyone for “ETIAS” now; the system is not open.
3. Keep a note of your days in the Schengen area so you stay within 90 days in any 180.
4. Before each trip, check the information on the [official EU portal](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/etias_en).
