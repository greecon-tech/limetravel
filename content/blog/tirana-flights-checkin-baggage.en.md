## How early should you arrive at Tirana airport?

Tirana’s Nënë Tereza airport has a current piece of advice on its passenger announcements page: arrive at least 3 hours before departure, citing high passenger numbers. The airport does not present it as a permanent rule, so it may change with the season and it is worth checking its page before every trip.

The check-in page gives no opening times. It only says that your desk appears on the flight details page and that some airlines require online check-in.

## Where do you check in, and until when?

The airport’s announcement mentions these practical details:

- **Ryanair:** from 10 November 2026, check-in and bag drop close 60 minutes before departure. Previously it was 40 minutes.
- **The common check-in service** for Ryanair, Wizz Air and Lufthansa Group runs 24 hours a day, up to 45 minutes before boarding.
- **Desks:** Ryanair in Hall A (desks A, B, 1, 2); Wizz Air and Lufthansa Group in Hall B (desks 14–24).
- **Hand luggage only:** passengers with a boarding pass on their phone can go straight to security.
- **Runway works:** night works from 9 to 26 November 2026 may change flight times.

## Which airlines appear on Tirana’s boards?

The flight boards on the airport website show Pegasus, Ryanair (including Ryanair UK), Wizz Air Malta and Aegean. We could not confirm other airlines, such as Air Albania, Lufthansa or Turkish, from the airport pages we read. That does not mean they do not fly; it only means we do not have it confirmed from this source.

## What is the liquids rule for hand luggage?

The Albanian Civil Aviation Authority (AAC), in a page updated on 9 November 2026, gives this rule: liquids in containers of up to 100 ml (or 100 g), all together in one transparent bag of up to 1 litre in total. Liquids bought duty-free after the boarding-pass check or after security are exempt, with conditions for sealed bags. The airport’s own carry-on page likewise says liquids over 100 ml are prohibited.

In the EU, the European Commission announced on 31 July 2024 that from 1 September 2024 airports with type-C3 scanners return to the 100 ml limit, as a precaution for a temporary technical issue. A Council document from June 2026 still refers to the lower limit. Some media report that certain airports allow up to 2 litres with newer scanners, but we found no official lifting of the limit across the EU, so keep to 100 ml.

## What to know about your airline’s baggage

Rules differ by airline and fare. Many basic fares from low-cost airlines include only a small bag, while a larger cabin bag or a checked bag is paid for separately. Sizes and weights are often shown differently on different pages of the same airline, so we give no figures here: they can change and we could not confirm them consistently.

Before you buy a ticket, open the baggage page of your chosen airline (links are in the sources below) and compare the total price with the baggage you need, not just the ticket price.

The airport stresses that all baggage is screened and publishes a list of prohibited items, such as explosives, pressurised gas or flammable liquids; medicines and alcohol are allowed only in limited amounts. See the list on the security page.

## Name and passport

The name on your ticket must match your travel document. Each airline has its own conditions and fees for changing a name, so check them before you confirm the booking.

On passport validity, the airport states in its notices: 90 days after return for Europe, 150 days from arrival for Turkey and 180 days for the UAE. This is the airport’s statement, not a government source, and rules vary by country, so always check the requirements of your destination.

## Checklist before you travel

1. Check the airport’s announcements page for timings and temporary changes.
2. Open your airline’s baggage page and confirm what your fare includes.
3. Check that the name on your ticket matches your passport and that your passport is valid for long enough.
4. Pack liquids in containers of up to 100 ml, in one transparent bag of up to 1 litre.
5. Check in online when the airline requires it and keep your boarding pass on your phone or printed.
