## Do Albanian citizens need a visa for the UK?

Yes. Albania is on the official list of countries whose nationals need a visitor visa to enter the United Kingdom. For a holiday, a visit to family, a short business trip or similar purposes, the relevant route is the **Standard Visitor** visa.

Do not confuse it with the ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation). The ETA is an electronic permission for nationals of countries that do not need a visa, and Albania is not on the ETA list. For Albanian citizens the ETA does not replace the visa.

## How long can you stay?

Usually up to 6 months. Medical and academic visitors have separate rules allowing longer stays, but those are different cases from tourism.

## How much does the visa cost and what are the options?

According to the Home Office fee table (published on 10 September 2026, effective from 8 October 2026), the visitor visa fees are:

| Validity | Fee (GBP) |
|---|---|
| 6 months | 135 |
| 2 years | 506 |
| 5 years | 903 |
| 10 years | 1,128 |

Longer visas do not let you stay longer on each visit: the stay is still usually up to 6 months per visit. Fees can change, so always confirm on the official source before paying. The visa application centre service fee has not been confirmed by an official page we could read, so we do not quote it here.

## How do you apply, step by step?

1. Complete the online application on gov.uk. You can apply up to 3 months before you travel.
2. Pay the official fee.
3. Book your appointment at the visa application centre, where you give fingerprints and a photo.
4. Upload or submit your supporting documents as instructed.
5. Wait for the decision. Gov.uk says you will usually get it within 3 weeks.

The British Embassy Tirana says visa enquiries are provided by VFS Global. Check the VFS Global page for Albania for the centre’s address and opening hours, as they change.

Each family member needs their own application, fee and appointment. Gov.uk allows you to apply on behalf of a partner or child who cannot apply for themselves.

## Which documents do you need?

Gov.uk asks you to show that you are a genuine visitor: that you will leave at the end of your stay, that you can support yourself and anyone travelling with you (or have someone funding you), and that you can pay for your return journey. The application covers your travel dates, accommodation details, expected costs and address history. Your travel history and employer details may also be requested.

The official guide to supporting documents says, among other things:

- **Finances:** bank statements showing where the funds came from. Statements or letters older than one year are not useful.
- **Employment:** a letter from your employer with your role, salary and length of employment.
- **Study:** a letter confirming enrolment and leave of absence.
- **Self-employed:** business registration or recent invoices.

## What about documents in Albanian?

Documents that are not in English or Welsh need a certified translation. The translation must include a confirmation of accuracy, the date, the translator’s full name and signature, and contact details, so that it can be independently verified.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Applying late: although a decision usually comes within 3 weeks, timing can vary, so start preparing several weeks ahead.
- Sending bank statements that are too old or that do not explain where the money came from.
- Leaving unexplained gaps in your travel or address history.
- Assuming an agency can guarantee approval. Gov.uk says documents do not guarantee success.
