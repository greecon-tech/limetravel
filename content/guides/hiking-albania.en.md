## Where can you hike in Albania?

Hiking in Albania is above all linked to the Albanian Alps in the north, but there are other areas. Llogara National Park, in the south, was established in 1966 among mountains with pine forests, and according to encyclopaedic sources its altitude runs from about 200 m to over 2,000 m. On the southern coast there are also trails, described in a GIZ document that the Albanian tourism agency has published.

## The Albanian Alps: Theth and Valbona

The Alps are the best-known hiking area. The Alps National Park, declared in 2022, covers more than 80,000 hectares and includes the Theth and Valbona valleys. The Theth–Valbona crossing is the best-known hike: about 14–16 km, about 6–8 hours, with a significant climb and a pass at roughly 1,760–1,850 m. The stage page rates it as moderate. More detail is on the page about the [Albanian Alps](/en/destinations/albanian-alps-theth-valbona).

## Peaks of the Balkans

Peaks of the Balkans is a 192 km circular route through Albania, Kosovo and Montenegro, with 10 stages, according to its pages. The recommended season is June to September. The border crossings require border permits, but sources differ on the procedure, so check the official source before you plan.

## When is the season?

In the Alps, the practical season is roughly June to September. Outside it there is a risk of snow, many guesthouses close and the road to Theth can be blocked by snow.

## Preparation

The Theth–Valbona stage page advises water, good boots and avoiding bad weather. Also bring clothing for changing weather and check the forecast before you set out.

## Safety

The general emergency number is 112, but in remote areas the response may be slow. Tell your host your route and do not hike in unsettled weather. We did not find official park rules published, so ask your host or the park administration. Respect the environment and do not leave rubbish.
