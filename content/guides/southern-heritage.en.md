## What are Berat and Gjirokastër?

Berat and Gjirokastër are two old Albanian towns, both part of the property “Historic Centres of Berat and Gjirokastra” on the UNESCO World Heritage List. They are valued for the architecture typical of the Ottoman period and for their well-preserved centres of vernacular buildings.

## What is there to see in Berat?

According to the Albanian tourism agency, Berat Castle is a 13th-century fortress on a rocky hill, one of the largest and best preserved in the country. Inside it are Byzantine churches such as St Michael, St Mary of Vlaherna and St Nicholas, along with houses and narrow streets. The castle houses the Onufri National Iconographic Museum, with icons including those of the 16th-century master Onufri.

The Mangalem and Gorica quarters are known for their cobbled lanes and white facades. Gorica lies on the other bank of the Osum river, with the churches of St Spyridon and St Thomas and the ruins of Gorica Castle.

## What is there to see in Gjirokastër?

Gjirokastër Castle stands above the town, with views over the Drino valley, and contains museums. The old town has an Old Bazaar with cobbled lanes and craft shops, and multi-storey stone tower houses, mostly from the 17th to 19th centuries, which earned the town the name “City of Stone”. The Ethnographic Museum is in the old town and displays furniture, costumes and tools.

## How do you get there from Tirana?

We did not find an official source for distances. Unofficial sources give about 100–125 km to Berat, about 2 hours, and about 157–160 km between Berat and Gjirokastër, about 2.5 hours by car. Information about buses from Tirana comes from commercial sources and may change; confirm at the terminal. We could not confirm the route and time from Tirana to Gjirokastër.

## When is the best time to go?

Guides usually recommend spring, June and September to October, because summer is hot and crowded. In winter some businesses outside the cities close, so plan carefully.

## What to taste

According to the tourism agency, Gjirokastër is known for dishes such as fërgesë, pashaqofta, qifqi and oshaf, as well as raki. Berat is known for kabuni, yaprak and the Tabosh Berati cake. The agency also mentions the Çobo and Nurellari wineries.

## Practical tips

Both towns have cobbled lanes and slopes, so allow time and wear suitable shoes. Opening times and ticket prices were not found published officially in the sources we read; check locally.
